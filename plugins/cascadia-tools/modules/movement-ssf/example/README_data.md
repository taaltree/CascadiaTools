# Example data: GPS-collared elk, summer 2024 (SIMULATED)

**These data are simulated.** No real elk were collared. The data were made by the
module's own simulator so that the true answer is known and the analysis can be
checked against it. Names, places and the "study" below are a story that makes the
example easy to follow.

## The story

A wildlife biologist collared 10 adult cow elk (*Cervus canadensis*) on a summer range
in the mountains of the inland Pacific Northwest (UTM zone 11N). Each collar took a GPS
fix every 2 hours from 1 May to 15 June 2024 (45 days). The biologist wants to know:

> Do elk choose forest and avoid high ground when they move, how strongly, and do
> individual elk differ? How big are their summer home ranges?

She also has two habitat maps of the study area: percent forest canopy cover and
elevation.

## Design

| dimension | value |
|---|---|
| animals | 10 elk (E01-E10) |
| fix schedule | every 2 h, 541 scheduled fixes per elk (540 steps), 1 May 00:00 - 15 Jun 00:00 UTC |
| fix attempts | 5,410 |
| failed fixes | 410 (7.6%), lost at random (not related to habitat) |
| timestamp jitter | 0-2 min after the scheduled time (as with real collars) |
| habitat covariates | forest canopy cover (%), elevation (m) |
| study landscape | 16 x 16 km, 50-m cells, UTM 11N (EPSG:32611), x 500,000-516,000, y 5,000,000-5,016,000 |

## Files

### `elk_gps_2024.csv` - one row per fix attempt

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `animal_id` | collar / animal id, E01-E10 |
| `timestamp` | date and time of the fix, `YYYY-mm-dd HH:MM:SS`, UTC |
| `utm_e` | easting (m), UTM zone 11N; empty when the fix failed |
| `utm_n` | northing (m), UTM zone 11N; empty when the fix failed |
| `dop` | dilution of precision (GPS quality; lower is better); empty when the fix failed. Not used by the model |
| `fix_type` | `3D`, `2D` (dop > 5) or `no fix`. Not used by the model |

### `forest_cover.tif` - forest canopy cover (%)

GeoTIFF, 320 x 320 cells of 50 m, EPSG:32611. Values 0-100; landscape mean 55.9%, SD 24.7.
Forest is partly lower on high ground (correlation with elevation is negative).

### `elevation.tif` - elevation (m)

GeoTIFF, same grid. Landscape mean 1,250 m, SD 220 m. Smoother (broader-scale) than forest.

### `config.yaml`

Tells ecotools which columns and rasters to use, the units for "meaningful change"
(10 percentage points of forest, 100 m of elevation), and the figure settings.

## TRUE values (simulated; known only because this is a simulation)

Generated by `dev/movement-ssf/make_example.R`:
`module$simulate(defaults$params, defaults$design + list(n_animals = 10, n_steps = 540, fix_rate = 1), seed = 2024)`,
then 8% of fix attempts blanked at random (`set.seed(99)`) to mimic failed fixes. The
selection coefficients are on the log scale per 1 landscape SD of the covariate.

| parameter | true value | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `sel_forest_mean` | 0.80 | population-mean selection for forest, per 1 SD (24.7 percentage points) |
| `sel_elevation_mean` | -0.50 | population-mean selection for elevation, per 1 SD (220 m) |
| `sel_forest_sd` | 0.40 | how much elk differ in forest selection (SD among animals) |
| `sel_elevation_sd` | 0.30 | how much elk differ in elevation selection |
| `rss_forest_per_sd` | exp(0.8) = 2.23 | relative selection strength for +1 SD forest |
| `rss_elevation_per_sd` | exp(-0.5) = 0.61 | relative selection strength for +1 SD elevation |
| `rss_forest_per_unit` | exp(0.8 x 10/24.7) = 1.38 | per +10 percentage points of forest |
| `rss_elevation_per_unit` | exp(-0.5 x 100/220) = 0.80 | per +100 m of elevation |
| `step_shape` | 2 | gamma shape of selection-free step length |
| `step_scale_m` | 120 m | gamma scale |
| `step_mean_m` | 240 m | mean selection-free step per 2 h |
| `turn_kappa` | 1 | von Mises concentration of turning angles (directional persistence) |
| `home_attraction_per_km2` | 0.25 | pull toward each elk's range centre (nuisance; not interpreted) |

Per-unit RSS truths use the landscape SDs of the example rasters (24.7 % and 220 m).

The realised sample of 10 elk has mean forest coefficient 0.70 (SD 0.35) and mean
elevation coefficient -0.56 (SD 0.32); the population values above are what the model
estimates. True home-range areas were not set directly (they come from the attraction
term and the movement kernel), so home-range estimates have no single true value.

## How the movement was simulated

Every 2 h, each elk drew 200 candidate steps from the selection-free movement kernel
(step length Gamma(2, 120 m), turn angle von Mises(0, 1) relative to its previous heading)
and chose one with probability proportional to
exp(beta_forest x forest_z + beta_elev x elevation_z - 0.25 x d^2), where `_z` are the
standardised raster values at the step end and d is the distance (km) to the elk's range
centre. 100 burn-in steps were discarded. This is exactly the process an integrated
step-selection analysis assumes.
